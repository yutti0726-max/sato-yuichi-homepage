The Golden Ratio PC Build Bible | Three Balanced Custom-PC Builds

Updated: October 25, 2026

Some prebuilt and build-to-order PCs pair a powerful CPU with a weak graphics card, or leave little power-supply headroom for a later upgrade. Written by a builder who has assembled more than 100 PCs, this short guide lays out three balanced custom-PC builds using parts sold in the US, with prices checked mostly in October 2026.

In short: pick the route that matches what you'll do most, check current prices and compatibility, and start from a balanced parts list instead of a blank page.

The Golden Ratio PC Build Bible: Three Balanced Custom-PC Builds and Desk Upgrades for 2026

Three builds for readers in the US — Route 1: Everyday & Upgrade-Ready (about $1,100), Route 2: 1440p Gaming (about $1,800), and Route 3: Creator & Local AI (about $3,000–3,500) — each with a parts table showing the model, an approximate price with where and when it was checked, and why it was chosen. It also covers what the 2026 jump in memory and SSD prices means for your budget, three desk upgrades, and a before-you-buy checklist. Prices are approximate and change often, and the book doesn't promise specific frame rates or performance.

What you'll get out of this book

Some prebuilt PCs look impressive on the spec sheet but spread the budget unevenly. Drawing on more than 100 real builds, this book explains why each part was chosen, so you can check the reasoning and adjust a route to your own needs. No parts list can guarantee a particular result, so check current prices and compatibility, for example with PCPartPicker, before you buy.

It's a short guide made up of text and tables (no photos). It is an English edition of a book first published in Japanese in July 2026, with the parts lists replaced by parts sold in the US and prices in US dollars.

FAQ

Q. What budgets do the builds cover?

A. Three routes, at about $1,100, about $1,800, and about $3,000–3,500, using US prices checked mostly in October 2026. Prices change often, so check current prices before you buy.

Q. Can I use the parts lists outside the US?

A. The parts lists use parts sold in the US, with prices in US dollars. If you live elsewhere, prices and availability where you live may differ.

Q. Does it cover peripherals too?

A. Yes — three desk upgrades: a mouse, a left-hand keypad, and a monitor arm.

Q. Will it help me judge a prebuilt or build-to-order PC?

A. Yes. It explains two common pitfalls — a powerful CPU paired with a weak graphics card, and a power supply with little headroom for a later upgrade — and why to ask for the exact model of each part in a quote.

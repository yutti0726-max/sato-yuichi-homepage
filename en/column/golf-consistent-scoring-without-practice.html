Breaking 100 Without Practice | What Matters More Than Distance in Golf

Updated: September 8, 2026

I've never played anything but business golf, and I don't go to the driving range. Even with that kind of golf history, I break 100 consistently and have shot in the 80s. Here's the thinking that keeps my score stable without relying on practice volume.

In short, prioritizing a stable swing that doesn't slice or hook over raw distance — then learning your real per-club distance — and sharpening your approach and putting accuracy once you're breaking 100, is what keeps your score consistent without practice.

Why does my score stay consistent without practice?

Honestly, I've never had much interest in golf itself — it's always been strictly business golf for me. I don't go to the driving range, and for the past five-plus years the only time I've held a club has been on an actual course. Even so, I break 100 consistently, and I've shot in the 80s. Without practice volume to lean on, what I've focused on instead is simply deciding what to prioritize.

What matters more than hitting the ball farther?

The first thing I try not to think about is distance. The harder you try to hit it far, the more your swing tends to break down, leading to slices and hooks. What should come first instead is building a swing that flies straight and stable. Only once that swing is stable can you actually know — club by club — how far you really hit the ball.

Once you have a stable swing that doesn't curve off-line, you start to know your real distance for each club, which makes course management much easier to plan. Practicing to hit it farther can always come later.

What's the key to making 100 stick?

If you're stuck once you start breaking 100, revisiting your approach shots and putting — rather than your swing — tends to stabilize your score without adding more practice. Even if you lay up a bit on a long hole, as long as your green-side play and putting don't fall apart, you avoid the blow-up holes that wreck a scorecard.

What does it mean to aim for the green?

It also matters not to get frustrated when a shot doesn't land where you aimed. Rather than aiming straight at the pin, switching your target to the whole green tends to bring your scores together. If you get on the green and finish in two putts, par is well within reach on both mid-length and short holes.

Do you have a favorite course?

Of all the courses I've played, Eniwa Country Club in Hokkaido is my favorite by far. Hokkaido summers don't get too hot, and the scenery is beautiful enough that I enjoy the round itself, not just the score. I try to appreciate the whole course experience, not just the number on the card.

Summary

Business golf only, no driving range — and your score can still be stable. What matters isn't practice volume, but prioritizing a stable swing over distance, and sharpening your approach and putting once you're breaking 100. Stop letting off-target shots frustrate you, and just switching your focus to the green can make your score noticeably more consistent.

FAQ

Q. Is it really okay to skip golf practice entirely?

A. I think staying conscious of swing stability while actually playing the course matters more than practice volume. That said, some minimum practice does help when building the basic swing itself.

Q. What should I prioritize first if I want to break 100?

A. Before trying to hit the ball farther, prioritize building a stable swing that doesn't slice or hook.

Q. What's the biggest reason scores stay inconsistent?

A. One big reason is getting frustrated when a shot doesn't go where you aimed. Simply shifting your target from the pin to the green tends to stabilize your score.

Q. Should I prioritize approach shots or putting?

A. Once you start breaking 100, improving the accuracy of your approach shots and putting does more for score consistency than more swing practice aimed at distance.

Q. Do you have a course you'd recommend?

A. Eniwa Country Club in Hokkaido is my favorite. The comfortable summer climate and beautiful scenery make a round genuinely enjoyable.

Q. What's the key to shooting par?

A. Aim for the whole green rather than the pin, and once you're on, focus on finishing in two putts. That mindset makes par a realistic target on mid-length and short holes.

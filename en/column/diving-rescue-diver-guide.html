What Is Rescue Diver? What the Certification Covers and What You'll Learn

Updated: September 7, 2026

"Rescue Diver" is a name that comes up a lot as the step after Advanced Open Water. It isn't required if all you want is to enjoy fun dives, but earning it is said to completely change how you see the underwater world. Here's what it takes to qualify and what it covers.

In short, Rescue Diver is a safety-management-focused certification covering self-rescue and how to help rescue other divers, and it's a great next step for anyone who's earned Advanced and logged a decent number of dives.

What Is Rescue Diver?

Rescue Diver is a PADI C-card level positioned above Advanced Open Water Diver. Beyond handling your own trouble (self-rescue), you systematically learn emergency-response skills — helping an exhausted or panicking diver, searching for a missing diver, and handling an unresponsive diver. It's a major turning point that expands your mindset from "enjoying diving for yourself" to "keeping an eye on your buddies' safety too."

What Are the Requirements?

  • Hold an Advanced Open Water Diver (AOW) certification
  • Have completed CPR/first aid training (such as EFR with PADI) within the past 24 months
  • A reasonable amount of logged experience (the guideline varies by shop, but around 20 dives is often recommended)

You can sometimes take it right after earning your AOW, but it's easier to focus on the course content once you're already comfortable with basic underwater skills.

What Do You Learn in Rescue Diver?

  • Self-rescue: how to handle a leg cramp, exhaustion, or the onset of panic yourself
  • Helping exhausted or panicking divers: how to approach, calm, and tow a diver calling for help on the surface
  • Searching for a missing diver: an organized procedure for searching for a buddy who hasn't returned within a set time
  • Handling an unresponsive diver: bringing them up from underwater and the procedure for towing them to shore or a boat while giving rescue breaths on the surface
  • Emergency management scenarios: practical scenarios simulating multiple problems happening at once, to build your judgment under pressure

Since it's centered on practical training in the pool and open water rather than just classroom learning, it's known as a physically demanding course. That's exactly why the sense of accomplishment and confidence on finishing it is often described as significant.

For me personally, experiencing night diving in Shakotan during my Advanced certification, and viscerally learning that "I myself could get into trouble underwater," was one of the reasons I decided to take Rescue Diver. I'm scheduled to take it on the 9th of this month, and I plan to write a separate report on how it actually goes afterward.

Why Do People Say It "Changes How You See" Diving?

Many divers say that through the Rescue Diver course, they became someone who "constantly keeps an eye on their buddy and other divers around them, not just themselves." You build habits like noticing air remaining or signs of trouble early, and catching small anomalies before they escalate — which, as a side effect, tends to make your own diving safer too.

What Can You Do After Earning It?

Rescue Diver is also a requirement for the professional-level Divemaster course. Even for those not aiming to go pro, it means becoming someone your dive buddies can genuinely rely on, and gaining the confidence to act calmly in an emergency — real benefits for continuing diving as a hobby.

Summary

Rescue Diver isn't required if all you want is to enjoy fun dives, but it's a certification well worth earning if you want to keep diving safely for a long time. Once you've earned Advanced and logged a decent number of dives, it's well worth considering as your next step.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Can I take Rescue Diver right after Advanced?

A. It's possible once you meet the requirements, but taking it after you've gotten comfortable with basic underwater skills makes it easier to focus on the course content.

Q. Do I need CPR certification to take it?

A. PADI's Rescue Diver course requires having completed CPR/first aid training (such as EFR) within the past 24 months. Some shops let you take that training at the same time if you haven't.

Q. Is Rescue Diver unnecessary if I just want to enjoy fun dives?

A. It's not required, but it's a certification well worth getting if you want to keep diving safely for a long time.

Q. Is the Rescue Diver course physically demanding?

A. It's known as a physically demanding course, since it's centered on practical training in the pool and open water rather than just classroom learning. That's exactly why the sense of accomplishment on finishing it tends to be large.

Q. What's the next step after Rescue Diver?

A. Rescue Diver is also a requirement for the professional-level Divemaster course. Even if you're not aiming to go pro, it builds skills that make you a diver others can rely on.

A Laptop + eGPU Setup for the Ultimate Space-Saving Desk | Everything Connects Through One Type-C Cable

Updated: September 7, 2026

I don't run local AI or heavy-graphics gaming as a daily habit, so a single lightweight laptop is plenty for everyday use. But once I get home, plugging in just one Type-C cable connects an eGPU, keyboard, display, mouse, and external SSD all at once, switching everything into the ultimate desk setup. Light on the go, no compromises at home — here's the record of building that setup.

In short, a lightweight laptop plus an eGPU docking setup lets you be as light as possible on the go, and switch to a desktop-class environment the instant you plug in one Type-C cable at home.

The Base: A Lightweight, Long-Battery-Life Laptop

My daily driver is an ASUS S14, with an Intel® Core™ Ultra 9 Processor 386H, 32GB of memory, and 1TB of storage. I don't run local AI as a daily habit, and I don't game at high graphics settings, so this spec runs more than comfortably enough for everyday use. I prioritized being lightweight and lasting a long time on battery over raw specs when choosing this model.

How Do I Switch to a High-Spec Environment at Home?

Once I'm back home, I use an external eGPU — the GIGABYTE AORUS RTX 5060 Ti AI Box. The biggest benefit of an eGPU (external graphics box) is that you carry around a lightweight laptop day-to-day, and connect to a stationary high-performance graphics card only when you need the extra power. Even the newest games or heavy workloads that a laptop alone can't handle become desktop-class performance once I'm home and routed through this eGPU.

How Does One Type-C Cable Connect Everything?

The best part of this setup is that connecting the laptop to the eGPU with just one Type-C cable activates all the other gear together. Getting home and doing exactly one thing — plugging in the cable — turns on all of the following:

  • External graphics card (GIGABYTE AORUS RTX 5060 Ti AI Box)
  • Keyboard and number pad
  • Main display
  • Mouse
  • External storage (SanDisk 1TB Extreme Portable SSD)

There's no more plugging in a pile of cables one by one like before — I can start working or gaming the moment I get home.

What's on the Desk?

Number Pad: EPOMAKER EK21 VIA Gasket Numpad

For a number pad, I use the EPOMAKER EK21 VIA Gasket Numpad Gaming Keyboard (Black Silver Wisteria V2). It's already fitted with the same silent switches and keycaps I covered in my keyboard mod article, giving a consistent feel and silence with my main keyboard.

Desk Extender: Sanwa Direct Folding Clamp-Type

To expand desk space, I added a Sanwa Direct desk extender (folding, clamp-mounted). It's convenient because it lets me extend my work area only when I need it, without replacing the desk itself.

Wrist Rest: Faluber Urushi-Lacquered Wooden Wrist Rest

To reduce strain on my wrists, I use Faluber's urushi-lacquered wooden wrist rest (wood palm rest, black). It stays comfortable even on days with a lot of typing, and moves smoothly, which makes a real difference on long work days.

Mouse: ATK FIERCE X

For a mouse, I settled on the ATK FIERCE X after trying around 50 others — it's the one that felt the most right. I have small hands and use a claw grip, and a mouse that fits both that grip and hand size well was surprisingly hard to find, which is what led to trying so many. If you also have small hands and use a claw grip, this is one I'd especially recommend.

Choosing a mouse based purely on "it's popular" or "the grip shape is supposed to be good" can leave you with something that doesn't fit your hand size or grip style. If you can, actually hold one and check whether it fits your grip (palm, claw, or fingertip) before deciding.

Display: Titan Army 24.5" Gaming Monitor

I use a Titan Army 24.5" gaming monitor as the main display, with the laptop's own screen as a secondary display. That two-screen setup keeps work efficient, and when I'm gaming I switch to showing only the Titan Army display. Rendering to the laptop's screen as well while gaming eats into the processing budget that would otherwise go to the game, so keeping the second display (Titan Army) as the sole output during gaming lets all the processing focus on the game.

What Do I Carry When I'm Out and About?

When I head out, I carry just this laptop and Logitech's MX Anywhere 3S for Business. This laptop, with its Intel® Core™ Ultra 9 Processor 386H, has excellent battery life, letting me keep working for long stretches on the go without worrying about power. I've managed to keep what I carry to a minimum without sacrificing how much I can get done.

What Are the Advantages of This Setup?

  • Space-saving: no need to own a stationary desktop PC — it becomes desktop-class only when I'm home
  • The ease of one cable: plugging or unplugging a single Type-C cable switches multiple peripherals together
  • Optimized for the use case: dual display for work, single display for gaming — processing power routes to whichever scene fits
  • Lightness on the go: the laptop and a mobile mouse alone are enough for long stretches of work
  • No compromise on gaming performance: thanks to the eGPU, there's almost no recent title I can't run

Summary

Two seemingly conflicting wants — "light and long-lasting on the go" and "uncompromising performance at home" — turned out to be compatible with a laptop + eGPU + consolidated docking setup. Rather than building a stationary desktop, switching your environment with one cable around a single laptop is a great option for anyone who wants a space-saving, flexible way to work and play.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. What is an eGPU?

A. It's an external graphics box that you connect to only when you need it, pulling power from a stationary high-performance graphics card while day-to-day you carry around just a lightweight laptop.

Q. Why does one Type-C cable connect everything?

A. The eGPU unit itself doubles as a hub for the keyboard, display, mouse, and external SSD, so connecting the laptop to the eGPU with a single Type-C cable activates all the other gear together.

Q. Should I use the laptop's own screen too while gaming?

A. Rendering to the laptop's screen as well while gaming eats into the processing budget that would otherwise go to the game, so a single-display setup on just the main monitor is recommended.

Q. What should I prioritize when choosing a mouse?

A. Fit with your hand size and grip style (palm, claw, or fingertip) matters more than specs. It's best to actually hold one and check before deciding.

Q. What do you carry when you're out and about?

A. Just the laptop itself and a mobile mouse (MX Anywhere 3S for Business). A laptop with good battery life is enough on its own for long stretches of work.

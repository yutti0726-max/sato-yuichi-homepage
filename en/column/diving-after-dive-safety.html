What to Watch After a Dive | Flight Timing & Health Management Tips

Updated: September 7, 2026

Diving safety isn't just about the time you spend underwater — it also needs to account for what you do after the dive. Anyone scheduling a dive at a destination followed by a flight home the next day should be especially careful. Here's a rundown of what to watch after diving.

In short, leaving 12-24 hours before flying and avoiding intense exercise, long baths, and alcohol after a dive significantly lowers your risk of decompression sickness.

Why Does What You Do After a Dive Matter?

While diving, water pressure causes more nitrogen from the air you breathe to dissolve into your body (your blood and tissue) than usual. As you ascend, the drop in water pressure lets this dissolved nitrogen gradually turn back into gas and leave your body — but if this process happens too abruptly, it can form bubbles in your blood vessels, leading to "decompression sickness (DCS)," which can cause joint pain, numbness, or in severe cases paralysis. For a while after diving, this nitrogen is still working its way out of your system, so you need to avoid situations that add further pressure drops (flying, traveling to high altitude) or actions that significantly change your blood flow (intense exercise, long baths).

How Long Should I Wait Before Flying?

As a general guideline within the range of recreational diving, the following wait times are recommended (these are only guidelines — they vary based on dive count and depth, so always follow your dive computer or the shop's instructions).

  • A single dive: wait at least 12 hours
  • Multiple dives in one day, or diving on consecutive days: wait 18 hours or more
  • Any dive involving a decompression stop: waiting 24 hours or more is advisable
Scheduling a dive on the last day of a trip can mean you can't secure this wait time and end up missing your return flight. When planning your itinerary, it's safer to schedule your last dive for the day before you depart or earlier.

What Should I Avoid After Diving?

  • Intense exercise: sudden changes in blood flow can promote nitrogen bubble formation
  • Long, hot baths or saunas: blood vessels dilate and blood flow changes, so avoid these right after diving and stick to a lukewarm shower
  • Alcohol: promotes dehydration and makes it harder to notice changes in how you feel, so keep drinking to a minimum right after diving
  • Traveling to high altitude: driving mountain roads or hiking carries the same pressure-drop risk as flying

What Are the Health Management Tips?

Staying hydrated before and after diving also helps your body clear nitrogen, so build a habit of drinking water regularly. And if you notice joint pain, skin itching or blotching, numbness, or severe fatigue after diving, don't self-diagnose even mild symptoms — consult your dive shop or a specialist organization like DAN Japan right away. Early response makes a big difference to how well you recover from decompression sickness.

Summary

How you spend your time after a dive matters just as much as your underwater safety precautions. Just covering the basics — leaving enough time before flying, avoiding intense exercise and alcohol, and staying well hydrated — significantly lowers your risk of decompression sickness. Stay alert right up to the end of your fun diving trip.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Do I need to be careful about flying even after just one dive?

A. Yes. Even after a single dive, waiting at least 12 hours before flying is the general guideline.

Q. Is it okay to take a bath after diving?

A. A long, hot bath or a sauna changes your blood flow, so it's best avoided right after a dive — stick to a lukewarm shower instead.

Q. What are the symptoms of decompression sickness?

A. Joint pain, skin itching or blotching, numbness, and severe fatigue are among them. Don't self-diagnose even mild symptoms — consult a specialist right away.

Q. Is it okay to dive on the last day of a trip?

A. You won't be able to secure the recommended wait time before flying, so it's safer to schedule your last dive for the day before you depart or earlier.

Q. Should I avoid drinking alcohol after diving?

A. Yes. Alcohol promotes dehydration and can make it harder to notice changes in how you feel, so it's best to keep drinking to a minimum right after a dive.

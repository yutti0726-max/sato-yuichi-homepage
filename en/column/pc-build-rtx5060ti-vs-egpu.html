Using an RTX 5060 Ti: Custom Desktop or External GPU (eGPU)?

Updated: October 3, 2026

My everyday setup is a laptop connected to an external GPU (eGPU). The same GeForce RTX 5060 Ti can also go straight into a desktop. Which one is right for you comes down almost entirely to one question: do you carry your computer around? This article puts the two setups side by side.

In short: if your computer never leaves the desk, build a desktop. If you use a laptop on the go and want more power when you get home, an eGPU is the better fit.

Which should you choose?

A custom desktop suits you if…

…your computer stays on the desk. With the GPU plugged in directly, it's easier to get its full performance, and you can upgrade the CPU, memory and storage separately later. For the same budget, a desktop tends to give you more performance per yen.

An eGPU suits you if…

…you want a light laptop when you're out and heavy work or gaming at home. The AORUS RTX 5060 Ti AI BOX connects over Thunderbolt 5 and also charges the laptop (up to 100 W), drives displays and works as a USB hub, so plugging in one cable when you get home switches your whole workspace over. I describe how I use it in A Laptop + eGPU Setup for the Ultimate Space-Saving Desk.

What to check before buying an eGPU

The performance you get depends on whether your laptop has a Thunderbolt 5 (or compatible) port. Always check your laptop's port specification before buying.

Notes on building and maintenance

  • Always switch the power off before plugging or unplugging graphics card or eGPU cables. I once broke hardware by plugging in a graphics card's power cable while the system was live (the story is here).
  • When gaming on an eGPU, turn off the laptop's own screen and show the game only on the external display, so the system can focus on the game.
  • An eGPU box has fans inside and collects dust too. Clean its air intakes regularly, just as you would a desktop.

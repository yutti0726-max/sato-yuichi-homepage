Better Darts Comes Down to Mental Game and Barrel Fit | The Power of Daily Grouping Practice

Updated: September 8, 2026

I formed a darts team and pushed my rating close to A-flight, and along the way I've thought a lot about what really drives improvement. Here's why the "mental game matters" advice you always hear turns out to be true, and why picking a barrel that fits you matters just as much.

In short, getting better at darts comes down to two things: the mental consistency that comes from throwing and grouping every day, and finding a barrel that genuinely fits your grip and throwing style.

What matters most in darts?

You hear "the mental game matters" constantly around darts, and having actually run a team — logo and all — I've come to agree completely. My rating topped out barely at A-flight, but even so, mental steadiness while throwing affects your score more than technique does. Throwing every day and keeping your grouping tight makes hat-tricks easier to land and steadily raises your overall scoring, too.

What is grouping practice, and why does it matter?

Grouping means throwing three darts with the same motion and the same timing, aiming to land them as close together as possible. I think of it less as focusing on one exact spot and more as checking whether you can reproduce your own throw every single time. Once your grouping stabilizes, that stability carries directly into more consistent scoring.

Grouping practice needs no special equipment and can be kept up in small doses every day. Improvement comes faster from being conscious of "am I throwing the same way each time" rather than just whether the dart landed where you aimed.

What difference does barrel choice actually make?

The thing I think matters most in darts is barrel choice. Barrels come in different shapes — torpedo, straight, and more — and you need to pick one that fits your own grip and throwing style. And above everything, what matters most is whether you can genuinely like how that barrel flies.

Barrel shapeCharacteristicsGood fit for
TorpedoThick at the center, tapering toward the tipPlayers who want release control
StraightRoughly uniform thickness throughoutPlayers who want a stable grip feel

How do you find the barrel that's right for you?

A barrel that fits you and that you genuinely grow attached to naturally makes you want to throw more, and want to hit your target more precisely. Rather than choosing on spec sheets alone, I put weight on actually throwing it a few times and seeing whether I like how it flies. Once you find a barrel you're attached to, practice itself stops feeling like a chore.

Summary

Improving at darts, in my experience, comes down to two things rather than just throwing more: daily grouping practice to steady your mental game, and finding a barrel that truly fits you. Start by throwing three darts with the same motion and the same timing, and see how tight a grouping you can land.

FAQ

Q. Is the mental game really that important in darts?

A. In my real experience running a team, mental consistency affects your score more than raw technique does.

Q. What exactly should I do for grouping practice?

A. Throw three darts with the same motion and the same timing, and practice getting them to land as close together as possible.

Q. Should I go with a torpedo-shaped or straight barrel?

A. Neither is inherently better — the basic idea is to choose based on whether it fits your own grip and throwing style.

Q. What matters most when choosing a barrel?

A. Beyond the specs, it matters whether you genuinely like how it flies when you actually throw it. That attachment naturally pulls you into practicing more.

Q. How much practice does it take to reach A-flight?

A. It varies by person, but throwing daily with grouping in mind steadily pushes your rating up over time.

Q. What's the benefit of joining a darts team?

A. Sharing a goal with teammates makes it easier to stay motivated than practicing alone in silence.

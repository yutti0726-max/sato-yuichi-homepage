Editing PDFs on Your Phone: Why iPhone PDF Apps Lean Toward Subscriptions and Many Android Apps Are Free

Updated: October 23, 2026

Handling PDFs for work almost every day, one gap between iPhone and Android keeps standing out: PDF editing apps. Wanting to do the exact same thing, on iPhone I often run into apps that need a monthly subscription, while on Android I can usually choose from free apps. Here's where that gap likely comes from.

In short: iPhone PDF editing apps tend to be subscriptions, while Android has many free, full-featured ones. The difference in developer registration fees is likely one of the reasons. That said, iPhone's built-in tools let you sign, write on, and fill in PDFs for free.

Why are so many iPhone PDF apps subscriptions?

Publishing on iOS means paying Apple $99 every year to keep your Apple Developer Program membership active. Ongoing subscription revenue tends to be a steadier way to recover that recurring cost than a one-time purchase, which is likely one reason so many iPhone PDF apps are subscriptions. Depending on the app, even functions like merging, compressing, and OCR may require a monthly subscription.

Why does Android have so many free, full-featured apps?

Registering as an Android developer, by contrast, costs $25, once. With no recurring fee to cover, ad-supported free apps are an easier business model to choose, and many apps offer merging, splitting, compressing, OCR, and handwritten signatures for free. Android also holds about two-thirds of the global smartphone OS market (about 67% as of August 2026, according to StatCounter). That larger audience is likely another reason developers build for Android. I go deeper into this developer-economics angle in Why I Recommend Android Over iPhone.

What can you do for free with iPhone's built-in tools?

iPhone also lets you do some PDF work for free, without adding an app. According to Apple's official documentation, you can:

  • Markup: write on a PDF or add a signature. You can save the signatures you create and reuse them on other documents
  • Preview: annotate a PDF (text, shapes, signatures), highlight or underline text, and fill in forms

If you only need to sign, make simple notes, or fill in a form, it's worth checking whether the built-in tools are enough before paying for a subscription app. I haven't checked how far the built-in tools go for other tasks, such as merging, compressing, or OCR.

* Based on Apple's iPhone User Guide (the pages on Markup, filling out forms and signing documents, and annotating in Preview; checked October 2026). Steps and labels may differ depending on your iOS version.

Editing PDFs on Your Phone: Why iPhone Means a Subscription and Android Is Free

Updated: September 13, 2026

Handling PDFs for work almost every day, one gap between iPhone and Android keeps standing out: PDF editing apps. Wanting to do the exact same thing, iPhone nudges you toward a monthly subscription, while Android hands you free options with no real limit. Here's where that gap comes from.

In short: because of how developer registration fees work, iPhone's PDF apps skew subscription, while Android has plenty of free, full-featured ones.

Why are so many iPhone PDF apps subscriptions?

Publishing on iOS means paying Apple $99 every year just to keep your Apple Developer Program membership active. Recovering that recurring cost is much easier with ongoing subscription revenue than with a one-time purchase. The result: even basic PDF functions — merging, compressing, OCR — often run into a paywall.

Why does Android have so many free, full-featured apps?

Registering as an Android developer, by contrast, costs $25 — once, ever. With no recurring fee to cover, ad-supported free apps become a viable business model on their own, and you end up with plenty of apps offering merging, splitting, compressing, OCR, and handwritten signatures for free. Android's roughly 70% share of the global smartphone OS market only adds to the incentive for developers to build there.

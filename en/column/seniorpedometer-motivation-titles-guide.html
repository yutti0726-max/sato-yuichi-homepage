Tips for Sticking with Walking | Use Titles, Notifications, and History to Avoid Giving Up

Updated: September 7, 2026

Started walking for your health, only to find yourself losing steam after a few days? That's a common experience, and the biggest reason is usually that the results aren't visible. This article shows how to use a pedometer app's built-in mechanics to keep a walking habit going without forcing it.

In short, three "visualization" features — rising titles, goal-achievement notifications, and step history — let you stack up small wins, making it much easier to stick with walking as a habit.

Why Is Walking So Hard to Stick With?

Walking doesn't produce a dramatic change in your body on day one or even after a few days. Repeating something whose results are hard to feel is, for most people, genuinely difficult to keep up. Giving up after just a few days isn't a sign of weak willpower — it usually means the results of sticking with it were never made visible in the first place.

How Does the Title System "Visualize" Your Progress?

Rakuraku Step Counter's title system raises your rank as your cumulative walking distance builds up. It's framed as a journey starting from Tokyo, passing through place names across Japan, aiming to "conquer" the whole country, with a new title unlocking every 100,000 steps. Because "how far to the next title" is shown as a concrete number, it's easier to feel that today's steps are actually leading somewhere.

How Should I Use Goal-Achievement Notifications?

You'll get a notification the moment your step count for the day crosses a milestone — 3,000 / 5,000 / 8,000 / 10,000 / 15,000 steps, and so on. Alongside the title system's longer-term goal, these notifications let you feel a small win each individual day. Start with a realistic goal, and once hitting a notification feels routine, gradually aim for the next milestone up.

Setting a high goal from the start tends to lead to days you can't hit it, which can backfire and make you want to quit. The trick is to start from a step count you can realistically reach in your everyday life, then raise the bar gradually once achievement notifications start to feel routine.

How Should I Use Step History for Reflection?

The history feature lets you look back through your daily step counts. Noticing patterns — "I walked more last week than this week," or "my steps drop on rainy days" — helps you connect your daily rhythm to your step count, and can suggest natural ways to walk a bit more without forcing it.

Can This Help Watch Over Family Members Living Far Away?

The app has no built-in feature to share records with family, but a title rank or a goal achievement gives the person using it something easy to talk about. Asking "how far up the titles have you gotten?" during a phone call or a visit can be a more natural way to check in than asking directly about their health.

Summary

Struggling to stick with walking usually comes down to results being hard to see. By making good use of three "visualization" mechanics — rising titles, goal-achievement notifications, and step history — you can stack up small wins and keep a walking habit going without forcing it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Why is it so hard to stick with walking?

A. A big reason is that the results aren't immediately visible. It's hard to feel the change from a single day's walk, so without something to visualize your records and sense of accomplishment, motivation tends not to last.

Q. What is the title system?

A. Your title (rank) goes up as your cumulative walking distance adds up. It's framed as a journey from Tokyo, passing through place names across Japan, aiming to "conquer" the whole country — a new title unlocks every 100,000 steps.

Q. When do goal-achievement notifications arrive?

A. A notification arrives the moment your step count for the day crosses a milestone such as 3,000 / 5,000 / 8,000 / 10,000 / 15,000 steps. It makes each day's small achievements easier to feel.

Q. How far back can I see my step history?

A. You can look back through your daily step records for a recent range of days, useful for reflecting on how much you've walked compared to last week or last month.

Q. Can this also help with watching over a family member who lives far away?

A. The app itself has no feature to share records with others, but since title rank and achievement notifications naturally motivate the person using it, asking "how far up the titles have you gotten?" on a phone call can become a natural way to check in, even from a distance.

Q. Is there a trick to keeping it up every day?

A. It helps to avoid setting a high goal from the start, and instead keep up a step count that's realistic for you. Stacking up small wins like titles and achievement notifications makes it easier to turn into a natural habit.

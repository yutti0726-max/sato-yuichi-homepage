How to Compress a Video File Size on Your Phone | What to Do When It Won't Send

Have you ever tried to send a video you shot on your phone over LINE or email, only to see "the file is too large"? This article explains how to shrink a video's file size using only your phone — no computer required — with resolution guidelines for different purposes.

Why Video Files Get So Large

A video's file size is determined mainly by three factors: resolution, bitrate, and length. Since most phones now shoot in Full HD (1080p) or 4K by default, it's not unusual for even a few minutes of footage to exceed several hundred MB or even 1GB. Because LINE and email both cap the file size you can send, trying to send high-quality footage as-is often gets rejected.

Three Ways to Compress a Video

1. Let the sending app auto-compress it

Apps like LINE will automatically lower the quality somewhat when you send a video, but you can't control how much it compresses, and the result can end up grainier than you'd like.

2. Re-export it with video editing software on a computer

This lets you fine-tune resolution and bitrate, but it requires transferring the file to a computer and operating editing software, so it's not something you can do quickly while out and about.

3. Convert it by specifying the resolution in a phone app

The easiest option is to convert the video to MP4 with a phone app by specifying the output resolution. Everything happens right on your phone, and the whole process takes just a few taps.

The longer the video, the larger the file, too. For videos over a few minutes, lowering the resolution alone may not shrink the file enough — trimming it down to just the part you need can also help.

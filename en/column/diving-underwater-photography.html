How to Take Underwater Photos While Diving | My Experience with the Insta360 Ace2 Pro

Updated: September 7, 2026

Diving into beautiful water is only half as rewarding to look back on later if you can't capture it in a photo or video. I use the Insta360 Ace2 Pro for underwater shooting, and it produces astonishingly good results. Here's why underwater photography is hard, and the basic tips for getting a clean shot.

In short, if you cover the basics — get close to your subject, keep the light behind you, and stabilize your neutral buoyancy — plus use an action camera built for underwater shooting, anyone can take beautiful underwater photos.

Why Is Underwater Photography Hard?

Underwater is a completely different shooting environment from land. The deeper you go, the less light reaches you, and red tones get absorbed first, so the whole image tends to look blue. Shooting while swimming also makes camera shake more likely, and a lot of gear needs a dedicated housing (waterproof case), all of which tends to give people the impression it's a hurdle to get into casually.

What Was My Experience With the Insta360 Ace2 Pro?

I use the Insta360 Ace2 Pro for underwater shooting, and it produces astonishingly good results. The strong stabilization you'd expect from an action camera, combined with a shooting mode that gets natural-looking color even underwater, means I can get footage and photos worth watching back without any special editing — that's the appeal. Its waterproofing is solid too, and between that and how easy it is to handle underwater, it's been a genuinely satisfying choice as a camera for documenting my dives.

Waterproof depth rating and whether a separate waterproof case is needed varies by type of action camera. Always confirm before buying that it matches the depth and style you'll be diving at.

What Are the Basic Tips for a Clean Shot?

  • Get as close as you can to your subject: color and sharpness fade the further away you are underwater, so getting close is the basic rule
  • Keep the light behind you: shooting with sunlight coming from behind you brings out more natural color
  • Don't stir up sand: kicking up sand from the bottom with your fins instantly clouds both your view and your photos
  • Shoot after stabilizing your neutral buoyancy: an unstable body position causes camera shake, so settle your posture first
  • Use underwater shooting modes and color correction: actively use features that bring blue-tinted colors closer to natural

Summary

Underwater photography looks intimidating, but with the right gear and the basic tips down, anyone can come away with a beautiful shot. Thanks to the Insta360 Ace2 Pro, I've been able to keep vivid records of the creatures I've met in Kerama and Shakotan. If you want to preserve your diving memories more richly, give underwater shooting a try.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Why do underwater photos come out looking blue?

A. The deeper you go, the less light reaches you, and red tones get absorbed first, so the whole image tends to look blue. An underwater color-correction mode can bring the colors closer to natural.

Q. Do action cameras need a dedicated case?

A. The waterproof depth rating and whether a separate waterproof case is needed varies by model. Check before buying that it matches the depth and style you'll be diving at.

Q. How do I prevent camera shake?

A. The basic rule is to stabilize your neutral buoyancy before shooting. An unstable body position is a major cause of camera shake. Using the camera's own stabilization feature alongside this helps too.

Q. What's the ideal distance from the subject?

A. Underwater, color and sharpness fade the further away you are, so the basic rule is to get as close as you can to your subject.

Q. Any tips for not stirring up sand while shooting underwater?

A. Kicking up sand from the bottom with your fins clouds both your view and your photos, so keeping stable neutral buoyancy and shooting from a distance off the bottom is recommended.

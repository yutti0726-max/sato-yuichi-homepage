Share Location on the Way Home and at Meetups, With Consent

Updated: November 27, 2026

After a quick "On my way home," have you ever wished you could see roughly where someone is? The same goes for finding a friend at a crowded meetup, or wondering whether a parent who lives far away has charged their phone. This article walks through situations where sharing location with family and friends can help, and the two things to agree on first: consent, and how to stop.

If you share location only with people who have approved you, and only while you have it turned on, you can use it just for the moments you need. It is worth checking together, at the start, how to turn it off.

[Ad] This article introduces Imadoko Share, an Android app developed by the author.

What should you agree on before sharing your location?

Location is very personal information. Before you start, talk about who you will share with, when, and for how long.

  • Who: share only with people you have both approved
  • When: turn it on only when needed, such as on the way home or on an event day
  • How to stop: make sure both of you know how to turn sharing off and how to disconnect

Imadoko Share builds these three points into how it works. You connect with an invite code (valid for 10 minutes, single use) or a QR code, and nothing, not even battery level, is visible until the other person approves. The approval screen reminds you to check the display name and approve only people you know.

Situation 1: "On my way home"

Typing a message when you are heading home late can be a hassle. One-to-one chat in Imadoko Share has quick phrases that you tap to put in the message box, such as "On my way home," "I've arrived" and "Can you pick me up?" (nothing is sent until you press Send).

While sharing is on, the other person sees your last known location and when it was last updated. That makes it easier to check roughly where you are without a phone call. When you get home, send "I've arrived" and turn sharing off if you no longer need it.

Updates can be delayed or missing because of signal, battery, power-saving settings or permissions, so check the update time shown as well.

Situation 2: Meeting up with friends on a trip or at an event

In a crowd, "Where are you?" can go back and forth many times. If you have both turned sharing on, you can see each other's last known location and update time on the map. Quick phrases also include "Running a little late," "Where are you?" and "Got it."

Battery level and whether the phone is charging are shared too. At 20% or below, the app highlights it as "Low," which can be a prompt to agree on a meeting point before someone becomes hard to reach.

If you only needed it for the day, turn sharing off for each person afterwards. If you do not plan to stay connected, you can also disconnect.

Situation 3: Keeping in touch with parents who live far away

Not quite a daily phone call, but you would like to keep an eye on how things are. With each other's consent, you can share location and battery level both ways. Seeing the battery level or charging status can also be a reason to ask, "Did you remember to charge your phone?"

Your parent needs to install the app and approve the connection themselves. When you visit, check these four things together. They are the items the app's Help screen, "If sharing stops often, check these," lists for when sharing stops partway:

  1. Set location to "Allow all the time"
  2. Allow notifications
  3. Turn on location for the device
  4. Exclude the app from battery optimization

Some devices have their own power-saving features that can still stop the app even with these settings.

When updates stop, the app shows what is confirmed separately from what is only a guess (for example, the battery may have run out). If they turn sharing off, it is shown right away as "Confirmed." If nothing arrives for a while (about 12 minutes), the app shows "No contact." A guess does not mean something has happened. If you are worried, contact them directly.

How do you stop sharing and delete your data?

Imadoko Share offers these ways to stop and to delete. Check them together before you start.

  • Stop for one person: turn off the sharing switch for that person. Your last shared location stays on their screen, but when you turn it off you can choose "Also remove my last location from their screen." They will see "Sharing off (turned off by them)."
  • Stop from the notification: a notification is always shown while your location is collected. Press "Stop" in it at any time.
  • Disconnect: you immediately stop seeing each other's location, battery and chat. A new invite code is needed to connect again.
  • Block: in addition to disconnecting, that person can no longer use your invite codes. You can unblock them in Settings.
  • Delete your account: "Delete account" in Settings removes your profile, location and the messages you sent, and ends your connections. Messages you sent in connections that were already ended or still pending may not be deletable from the app.

Location is shared only when the other person has approved and a notification is shown while location is collected. There is no hidden mode. There are no ads and no usage analytics, and the app does not access your contacts. You can also record your own routes if you choose; they stay on your device and are never uploaded or shared.

FAQ

Q. Can I see someone's location without them knowing?

A. Location is shared only when the other person has approved and a notification is shown while location is collected. There is no hidden mode. You connect with an invite code or QR code, and nothing is visible until the other person approves.

Q. How do I stop sharing?

A. Use the switch for each person or "Stop" in the notification, at any time. When you stop, you can also remove your last location from their screen. You can disconnect or block as well.

Q. Does the other person need the app too?

A. Yes. Both of you need to install Imadoko Share and connect. At the time of writing, the app is available for Android (Google Play). You can send where to get it and how to start with "Invite family and friends" in the app.

Q. If updates stop, does it mean something has happened to them?

A. Not necessarily. Updates also stop with no signal, an empty battery, or power-saving settings. The app shows confirmed information separately from guesses. In an emergency, contact them directly or call emergency services.

Q. Can I use it to look out for my children?

A. Imadoko Share is intended for people aged 18 and over.

Q. Are there ads or usage analytics?

A. No. The app does not access your contacts either.

How to Scan Paper Documents into a PDF on Your Phone | Automatic Cropping & Deskewing

Updated: September 7, 2026

Contracts, receipts, a notice from school — there are plenty of times you want to submit or save a paper document as a PDF. You don't need to get hold of a scanner: your phone's camera can turn it into a PDF for you.

In short, the free Android app "PDF Toolkit" turns a camera shot into a deskewed, cropped PDF, and can combine multiple pages into a single file.

Can I Turn a Document into a PDF Without a Scanner?

Yes, you can. Even without a dedicated scanner, you can photograph a document with your phone and have it automatically deskewed and cropped on the spot, producing a PDF that looks like it came off a real scanner. It's especially handy when you're out and about and suddenly need to submit a document — your phone alone gets the job done.

How Do I Scan a Document into a PDF in PDF Toolkit?

  1. In PDF Toolkit, select the scan feature
  2. Point the camera at the document and capture it. Keep going for additional pages if needed
  3. Check the automatically detected document outline, and adjust it by hand if needed
  4. Save the deskewed, cropped pages together as a PDF

Because the whole flow — from capture to save — happens in one continuous sequence, you can combine several pages into a single PDF without getting confused, even for multi-page documents.

For a clean scan, shoot from a position where all four corners of the document are visible, in a bright location with no shadows falling across it. This improves the accuracy of the automatic outline detection, and the deskewing that follows.

How Do I Combine a Multi-Page Document?

Whether it's a stack of receipts or a contract spanning several pages, you can keep adding pages one photo at a time and save them all together as a single PDF at the end. There's no need to save each page as a separate file and merge them by hand afterward.

How Is Scan Different from "Create PDF from Photos"?

These may look similar, but they play different roles. The scan feature launches your camera on the spot and automatically corrects a trapezoidal, angled shot into a proper rectangle before turning it into a PDF — it's built for paper documents right in front of you. "Create PDF from photos", on the other hand, takes photos already saved in your camera roll and combines them into a PDF as-is, with no correction applied. Use the scan feature for a paper document that's in front of you right now, and "Create PDF from photos" for photos you've already taken.

What Can I Do After Scanning?

  • I want to make the text searchable and copyable: run OCR to recognize the text
  • I want to combine several scan results into one: use the merge feature
  • I want to fix a page's orientation or pull out just some pages: use rotate and extract

Summary

A paper document can become a PDF with just your phone's camera, no scanner required. With automatic cropping and deskewing, even a multi-page document can be turned into a single, clean PDF on the spot. Give it a try — it's handy for those sudden documents you need to submit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Can I turn a paper document into a PDF without a scanner?

A. Yes. You can turn a document into a PDF just by photographing it with your phone's camera, no dedicated scanner needed.

Q. What if my photo comes out at an angle?

A. The scan feature automatically detects the document's outline and corrects a trapezoidal, angled shot into a proper rectangle before turning it into a PDF. Shooting somewhat off-angle isn't a problem.

Q. Can I combine a multi-page document into a single PDF?

A. Yes. You can keep adding pages one photo at a time, then save them all together as a single PDF at the end.

Q. What's the difference between Scan and "Create PDF from photos"?

A. The scan feature launches your camera on the spot and automatically deskews and crops the document before converting it to a PDF. "Create PDF from photos" takes photos already in your camera roll and combines them into a PDF as-is, with no correction applied.

Q. Can I search the text of a scanned PDF afterward?

A. A PDF right after scanning can't be searched, but OCR can convert it into a state where the text is searchable and copyable.

Q. Is the scanned document sent to any server?

A. Processing happens entirely on-device, so even documents containing contracts or personal information are never sent to an external server.

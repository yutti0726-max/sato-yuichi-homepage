Does a Pedometer App Drain Your Battery? Why It Needs "Physical Activity" Permission

Updated: September 7, 2026

When you install a pedometer app, you may see a prompt asking you to allow access to "Physical Activity" and wonder what it's for. Here's why this permission is needed, and whether keeping it running in the background is safe for your battery.

In short, the "Physical Activity" permission lets the app read your phone's built-in step-counting sensor — it isn't continuous GPS tracking, so it uses far less battery than most people assume.

What Is the "Physical Activity" Permission For?

Today's smartphones have a dedicated step-counting sensor built in. A pedometer app reads the accumulated value from this sensor to display your step count. The "Physical Activity" permission is what lets the app access this sensor's data through Android. Without it, your step count will stay at zero even when you open the app.

Does Running It in the Background Drain the Battery?

This is the point that worries a lot of people, but many pedometer apps work quite differently from GPS-based location-tracking apps. GPS calculates your current location continuously using radio signals, which uses a lot of battery. A pedometer, on the other hand, simply reads the accumulated value from a low-power, built-in step sensor at intervals. Because of this, the impact on your battery is much smaller than an app that uses maps or navigation continuously.

If you're curious how much battery a pedometer app is actually using, check Settings → Battery → per-app battery usage on your device. Comparing it against an app that uses GPS constantly makes the difference clear.

What If the Permission Button Doesn't Respond?

If you once tapped "Don't allow," or if your phone's "remove permissions for unused apps" feature automatically revoked it after a period of inactivity, the in-app permission button may stop showing a dialog at all when tapped. This is by Android's own design — once a permission has been explicitly denied, an app can no longer trigger the system dialog again on its own. In that case, you need to grant the permission directly from your device's Settings.

How to Grant the Permission from Settings

  1. Open Settings on your device, go to the app list, and select Rakuraku Step Counter
  2. Select "Permissions," then open "Physical activity"
  3. Switch it to "Allow"
  4. Return to Rakuraku Step Counter and step tracking will start

Also Watch Out for Battery-Saving Features

Even with the permission properly granted, your phone's "Battery optimization" feature can occasionally pause a background tracking app. Excluding the pedometer app from battery optimization keeps tracking stable day after day.

Summary

The "Physical Activity" permission for a pedometer app exists to read your phone's built-in sensor — it doesn't continuously track your location the way GPS does. The impact on battery life is limited, so feel free to grant it and make step tracking part of your daily routine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. What is the "Physical Activity" permission for in a pedometer app?

A. It's the permission that lets the app read your phone's built-in step sensor. Without it, the app can't automatically count steps in the background.

Q. Does keeping a pedometer app running all the time drain the battery quickly?

A. Pedometer apps don't continuously determine your location the way GPS does — they read the accumulated value from a low-power, built-in step sensor. Because of this, the impact on your battery is smaller than typical GPS-based apps.

Q. Why doesn't the permission button respond sometimes?

A. If you've denied the permission once, or it was automatically revoked by your phone's "remove permissions for unused apps" feature, the in-app button may no longer be able to show the permission dialog. In that case, grant it directly from your device's Settings.

Q. What happens if the permission is turned off?

A. Automatic step tracking stops. In rare cases the background tracking service may also fail to start properly without the permission, so it's best to keep it granted at all times.

Q. Is location permission also required?

A. Location isn't used for counting steps itself. Depending on your device and Android version, a prompt related to accessing the step sensor may appear, but the app doesn't use GPS to track your whereabouts.

Q. Can a battery-saving feature stop the app?

A. If your phone's "Battery optimization" feature is turned on, it can occasionally pause background tracking. Excluding Rakuraku Step Counter from battery optimization keeps tracking stable.

Android Apps

Useful Free Android Apps, Sorted by What You Need

I'm Yuichi Sato, an independent developer based in Hokkaido, Japan, and this page lists the Android apps I have planned and built myself. I like to turn small everyday "wish this existed" moments into apps, like making a PDF, converting a video or copying text from a screenshot, and I only build what I find useful in my own life. Every app here is free. Whether an app shows ads, and whether a one-time purchase hides them, is noted for each app below. Four of the seven are live on Google Play, and three are still being prepared for release. All of them are Android-only and do not run on iPhone (iOS). Browse by task below, or jump to the full list.

PDFs and Documents

Need to turn paper documents into a PDF, combine several PDFs into one, or shrink a file that's too big to send? That's what PDF Toolkit is for. Documents you photograph are cropped and straightened automatically, and you can merge, split, compress, run OCR and add a handwritten signature, all on your phone.

Price: Free (with ads; PDFs exported on the free plan carry a small watermark, and a one-time PRO purchase removes both the ads and the watermark)

Video and Image Conversion

A video too big to send by message or email, a file that won't play and needs to become an MP4, an image you want to resize or convert between JPEG, PNG and WEBP. MediaToolbox puts video, image, audio and PDF conversion into one app, so you can do it on your phone without a computer. It can also pull just the audio out of a video.

Price: Free (with ads; a one-time "Upgrade to PRO" purchase hides all ads)

Reading Text on Screen (OCR)

For when retyping text from your screen feels like a chore. Readshot reads the text automatically as soon as you take a screenshot, and lets you copy, share, web-search or read aloud just the part you select. Recognition happens on your device, and images and recognized text are not sent to an external server (only when you run a web search is the selected text used for that search). To make the text in a scanned PDF searchable, use the OCR in PDF Toolkit.

Price: Free (with ads)

See Readshot's full features and how to use it →

Walking and Step Counting

For anyone who gave up on step-counter apps because they had too many buttons, or for family members setting one up on a parent's phone. Once you allow the "Physical activity" permission it needs to count steps, Rakuraku Step Counter records your steps automatically, without opening the app, and shows them in large text in a home-screen widget. Step Banking, which doesn't punish a day you couldn't walk, and one-tap sharing of today's steps with family are included too.

Price: Free (a banner ad at the bottom of the screen; no in-app purchases)

See Rakuraku Step Counter's full features and how to use it →

Baby and Parenting Records

For parents who want to keep each day's photos and moments. HagLog brings a photo diary, height, weight and head-circumference charts, vaccination and check-up records, and handprints and footprints into one app, and can export them as a single memory-book PDF with a cover. Your records stay on your device only, and no account is needed. It is meant to complement your paper baby record book, not replace it.

Price: Free (with ads; a one-time "Remove ads" purchase hides them). Still being prepared for release, so the Google Play link may not open until it goes live.

See HagLog's full features and how to use it →

Data Usage and Phone Status

For anyone worried about using up their mobile data. GigaPulse shows today's and this month's data usage in a home-screen widget, and a per-app ranking plus alerts as you near your limit make overuse easier to spot. It can also show CPU and memory usage, and its memory release asks Android to end background processes of apps you aren't using (the effect is temporary). It is not a storage cleaner and does not delete cache or junk files.

Price: Free (with ads; a one-time PRO purchase removes them). Still being prepared for release, so the Google Play link may not open until it goes live.

See GigaPulse's full features and how to use it →

Location Sharing with Family and Friends

For sharing "Where are you?" and "Is your battery OK?" with friends you're meeting, family who live apart, or your partner. In Imadoko Share you connect with an invite code or QR code, and nothing is visible until the other person approves. You can stop sharing with each person at any time, and a notification is always shown while your location is being collected (there is no hidden mode). It is not an emergency service and cannot guarantee anyone's safety. It is intended for people aged 18 and over.

Price: Free (no ads, no usage analytics). Still being prepared for release, so the Google Play link may not open until it goes live.

See Imadoko Share's full features and how to use it →

Using These Apps with Peace of Mind

  • Who makes them: Every app on this page was planned and built by me, Yuichi Sato. To be clear, this page promotes my own apps.
  • Ads: Six of the seven apps show ads; Imadoko Share does not. Where and what kind of ads appear is explained in the FAQ on each app's page (the question about whether it is free).
  • Permissions: Each app page explains which permissions a feature uses and why. For example, Rakuraku Step Counter uses "Physical activity" to count steps, and GigaPulse uses "Usage access" to total your data usage. Imadoko Share asks for "Allow all the time" location access so it can share while the app is closed; if you don't allow it, you can still share while the app is open.
  • Your data: Text recognition in Readshot, the diary, photos and growth records in HagLog, and the data-usage figures in GigaPulse are handled on your device and are not sent to an external server (as stated on each app page). In Imadoko Share, your location and battery level are shared only with people you have approved, and only while sharing is on, and you can delete your Imadoko Share account and data from within the app at any time.
  • About location sharing and the watch-over alert: The optional watch-over alert in Rakuraku Step Counter (an SMS to a registered number on days with zero steps) and Imadoko Share do not guarantee anyone's safety. Depending on signal, battery and device settings, messages and updates may be delayed or may not arrive.
  • About this website: This site's privacy policy covers analytics, ads and affiliate links on the website. For how each app handles data, please check its app page as described above.

FAQ

Q. Are the apps on this page free?

A. Yes, every app is free. Six of the seven show ads (Imadoko Share does not), and MediaToolbox, PDF Toolkit, HagLog and GigaPulse let you hide the ads with a one-time purchase (in PDF Toolkit it also removes the watermark on exported PDFs). Rakuraku Step Counter and Readshot have no in-app purchases. Please check each app's page and its Google Play listing for details.

Q. Do these apps work on iPhone?

A. No. All seven are Android-only apps.

Q. Can I download all of them right now?

A. MediaToolbox, PDF Toolkit, Rakuraku Step Counter and Readshot are live on Google Play. HagLog, GigaPulse and Imadoko Share are still being prepared for release, so their Google Play links may not open until they go live.

Q. Are my photos or recognized text sent anywhere?

A. As stated on their app pages, text recognition in Readshot and the diary, photos and growth records in HagLog are handled on your device and are not sent to an external server (when you run a web search in Readshot, the selected text is used for that search). GigaPulse also processes your data usage only on your device.

Q. Who makes these apps?

A. I, Yuichi Sato, plan and build them myself as an independent developer. See my profile page for more.
